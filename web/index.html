Plain answers

What is an AED?

An automated external defibrillator is a portable box that checks the heart's rhythm and, if it finds one that a shock can fix, delivers that shock. It speaks each step out loud. You do not need training to use one, and it will not shock someone who does not need it.

Why does the time of day matter?

Because a defibrillator inside a locked building cannot be fetched, and the shock has to come before the ambulance does. In a large US study of witnessed public cardiac arrests with a shockable heart rhythm, 66.5% of people shocked by a bystander with a public AED survived to leave hospital, against 43.0% of those who waited for the ambulance crew (Pollack et al., Circulation, 2018).

Is this an emergency service?

No. It is a planning tool for cities, schools and residents. In an emergency, call 911 first: dispatchers can talk you through CPR and send help.

Where do the opening hours come from?

New York's AED registry has no hours, so we built them, and every location says where its hours came from. {{nyc:findings.schoolLocations|int}} schools use the public-school calendar. {{nyc:hoursSources.places|int}} venues use the opening hours Amazon Location Service publishes for them. For {{nyc:hoursSources.ai|int}} more, Amazon Bedrock made an estimate that we show but never count as open in the headline numbers. {{nyc:hoursSources.unknown|int}} remain unknown.

How good are the AI estimates?

We tested them blind on {{nyc:aiGrade.venuesWithKnownHours|int}} venues whose real hours we know. They agreed on {{nyc:aiGrade.hourAgreement|pct}} of the hours in a week, but called a venue open when it was shut {{nyc:aiGrade.falseOpenShare|pct1}} of the time. That error points the dangerous way, so the headline numbers leave AI estimates out. You can switch them on in the map.

How is "covered" measured?

Amazon Location Service routed a two-minute walk from each of the {{nyc:findings.locations|int}} locations along real streets. We laid a 50-metre grid over the city, counted which grid squares fall inside an open AED's walking area at each hour of the week, and weighted each ZIP code by the cardiac-arrest calls that came from it at that hour.

Can I check my own city?

Yes. Upload a list of places with opening hours on the Your data page, or call the API. France already makes every AED owner declare hours: in Paris, AEDs open by their declared hours fall from {{paris:figures.openTue2pm|int}} on a Tuesday afternoon to {{paris:figures.openSun3am|int}} at 3 AM on Sunday.
